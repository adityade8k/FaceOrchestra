# Kuch To Hua Hai

Open **Tutorials → Kuch To Hua Hai**, choose **Easier bends** (the default) or **Original**, then **Start Tutorial**. The same choice appears under **Record Songs → Kuch To Hua Hai → Prepare Recording**. The selection stays fixed throughout the session and is shown in the lesson/recording panel. To compare the other arrangement, open the song's library entry again.

Original has nine exercises: D-major accompaniment, C-to-D accompaniment, stick groove, a switch-and-return exercise, four melody lessons, and **All together**. Easier bends adds a short demonstration/practice introduction before the first melody drill. Melody practice and demonstrations prepare missing backing immediately through the shared composition compiler. The full simulation can still demonstrate the accompaniment recording exercises. Existing compatible learner takes are reused; replacements are snapshotted.

Section definition version 2 uses performance ranges **[0,16), [16,40), [48,88), [96,136)**: 22, 39, 61 and 63 source notes. Every source note belongs to exactly one section; no hold or bend crosses a boundary. Section practice adds an explicitly labeled eight-beat practice-only bridge after Part 1 and uses the genuine eight-beat interludes after Parts 2 and 3. Backing continues through each break; melody/gauge targets disappear. Each subsequent attempt requires Start Practice and a fresh count-in. Full-song practice and recording join Parts 1 and 2 directly and retain only the real rests at [40,48) and [88,96).

The authored song tempo remains 92 BPM. Melody practice and demonstrations use the selected session tempo, initially 60 BPM, with a quiet metronome and four-beat count-ins. Accompaniment exercises and Record Songs use 92 BPM. All three backing loopers record fixed four-bar (16-beat) windows with no extra gap. Original retains all 185 notes from the MIDI's **Guitar** track, including their pitches, durations and velocities; the two sixteen-beat interludes remain shortened to eight beats. Both arrangements last 136 performance beats (88.696 seconds at 92 BPM, 136 seconds at 60 BPM), preserving the accompaniment and full-song pattern switches.

Easier bends contains **167 squeeze gestures covering all 185 source pitch landmarks**. Eighteen approved adjacent pairs become held slides: sixteen ascend two semitones and two descend one semitone. This deliberately replaces two attacks with one continuous voice and bridges the one-tick source gap. Repeated attacks, genuine rests, fast reversals, ornaments, large leaps and other notes remain separate. Fewer gestures are not proof of improved physical comfort or musical phrasing; listening and headset review remain necessary.

## Practice

- **Demonstrate** plays the current exercise; **Practice** starts a learner attempt. The same button stops an active attempt. **Next Step** skips an exercise.
- On desktop, hold the labeled note/chord buttons to squeeze and release steady pitches. Focus a button and hold Space or Enter to use the keyboard. Use XR for held-bend practice; Demonstrate also works on desktop. Tap **Boink** or **Hihat** to move the simulated stick through the real collision target and withdraw it.
- In XR, aim at a Honk's bottom sphere and hold Trigger. Touching Honks form each chord. Keep the wrist neutral for ordinary notes. For an authored slide, establish the starting pitch, keep the same Trigger held on the same Honk, follow the roll marker toward the destination, settle, then release. Grip in empty space equips the stick; withdraw after each strike.
- Practice on each accompaniment step records an actual take. D and C-to-D patterns live in separate physical Loopers sharing one Metronome output, with one recording per Looper. Re-recording replaces that Looper’s recording. Percussion uses a different output for simultaneous playback. **Practice full song — backing ready** is available at song entry and in lesson navigation; it prepares missing patterns immediately without awarding lesson credit or starting an MR take. Melody exercises stay live and do not record another looper.
- Feedback compares observed learner pitches, attack timing, continuous voiced holds, processed bends, destination landing/settling, releases and stick withdrawals. Ordinary notes are also checked for unintended bends. Examples do not earn learner credit. Recordings remain available even when the score is imperfect; cancelling a recording restores the previous take.

A fixed white circle marks each upcoming or sounding target. During the one-beat lead-in, green steadily shrinks to zero at the attack. Yellow starts at zero at the attack and grows outward throughout the hold, disappearing when it reaches the white circle at the scored release. Active and nearest upcoming actions take priority over fading releases. Repeated countdowns on the same target are coalesced to the nearest attack. A slide's radial gauge stays at its starting Honk's authoritative squeeze sphere and follows movement/scale. The radial gauge appears during green preparation with its target held at the starting pitch and “You —”. Its target turns yellow at the attack and starts rotating only after the initial authored hold, while the yellow ring grows. The hollow triangle shows the authored target now; a filled circle on a separate radial lane and signed arc show the correct held controller's processed live bend, the same value used by audio. Signed ±4 ticks, a zero marker, destination notch and pitch-pair/numeric labels distinguish target, actual and destination. Missing input reads “You —”; wrong direction, overshoot and “On target” use the authored cents tolerance. Demonstration reads “Demo” and earns no learner credit. The wrist projection uses the controller/view orientation. Meshes, geometries, materials and label canvases are pooled; numerical text redraws only when changed, at most every 100 ms except ownership transitions. These musical rings remain visible when the main menu is hidden and remain excluded from clean capture. Explicit UI capture includes guidance. The controller capture-status badge follows main-menu visibility immediately; its status continues updating while hidden.

The prepared ensemble and takes belong to the lesson session. **Exit** restores the previous free-play scene. Examples preserve learner takes. Hiding the tab, losing focus, exiting XR, or a long frame interruption stops the exercise; restart with a fresh count-in. The normal frame loop drives the conductor, so the lesson owns no scheduling timers. At the final musical boundary all backing loopers stop and voices release. Section practice stops backing at the end of its eight-beat break.

## Tempo and progress

The four melody sections and full-song section each own **40 → 50 → 60 → 70 → 80 → 92 BPM** progress. New sections still start at 60; select 40 or 50 for slower practice. Earlier saved progress is retained, without marking the new slower stages complete. Progressive practice is enabled by default. A stage passes with at least 85/100 and all required bend/hold/release criteria (`PROGRESSION_POLICY`); elapsed time and demonstrations never award completion. Success stores the actual attempt BPM and selects the next stage without starting it. Failure retains the attempted BPM. Manual slider/preset selection and Next Step do not fabricate completed stages. Persistence includes composition version, arrangement ID/version, section-definition version, section ID and BPM; old section progress is isolated.

The desktop native range spans 40–92 BPM in steps of one, with presets at 40, 50, 60, 70, 80 and 92, and accepts keyboard input. The XR slider maps controller rays into panel-local coordinates, captures Trigger until release, clamps outside its track and commits once. Hide, tracking reset/loss, disconnect and exit cancel an unfinished drag. A committed tempo change cancels an active practice attempt without a failure, clears scheduled backing/voices, and restarts that section with a fresh four-beat count-in. Takes, arrangement and placement survive. Idle/result changes configure the next attempt. Direct metronome changes use the same action; an active MR take cannot change practice tempo.

An immutable per-attempt timing context drives demonstrations, cues, bends, scoring, backing switches, interludes and completion. Source beats are never rescaled. The existing looper source-tempo/playback-tempo conversion keeps prepared or recorded patterns sixteen beats long without changing pitch.

## Musical source

`src/tutorial/kuch/midiReference.js` contains note tuples extracted from the supplied `m4-gtr35-kuch-to-hua-hai.mid`: ticks, duration ticks, MIDI pitch/identifier and velocity at 480 ticks per beat. The Classical Guitar supplies D3/A3/D4/F♯4 and C3/E3/G3/C4/E4 voicings and their rhythm. Drumset beat 24–40 supplies twelve bass-drum and eight snare hits, adapted to the existing boink and hihat stick sounds. Drum identifiers never become pitched Honks. Chord strums receive a short release gap so their gates remain distinct at normal display frame rates.

The immutable MIDI reference and original `MELODY` stay in `midiReference.js` and `score.js`. `arrangements.js` derives version 1 of each choice with source IDs, timing landmarks, gesture spans, curves and articulation changes. The approved pairs are `3+4, 14+15, 41+42, 52+53, 61+62, 64+65, 72+73, 75+76, 83+84, 102+103, 113+114, 126+127, 128+129, 138+139, 140+141, 146+147, 165+166, 176+177` (all IDs have the `lead-` prefix). Import-time checks reject changed pitches, timings, gaps or drill membership. Demo, practice, performance and recording guidance consume this same derived data. Composition version 2 and per-arrangement progress keys prevent old mastery records from implying mastery of these changes. New takes include arrangement ID/version and the selected composition hash; historical capture replay remains unchanged.

Each candidate curve starts rolling 0.20 beats before the destination landmark and commands its endpoint 0.04 beats after it, leaving starting and destination holds. Learner scoring uses observed base MIDI plus processed bend: 20 cents for base tuning, 35 cents for sounded pitch, 115 ms onset/release bounds (short ordinary notes have tighter release bounds), an 80 ms starting hold, a 110 ms destination hold, and a landing window from 65 ms before to 180 ms after the source landmark. The live roll range and held-gesture smoothing are unchanged. A fresh controller attack resets residual bend from a released gesture, without resetting another hand's continuing hold or its release tails. This matters where B4→C♯5 is followed by a separately attacked B4. Browser checks also sample actual oscillator frequency/detune parameters, allowing their additional smoothing up to 225 ms after the landmark. These parameter checks do not substitute for acoustic listening.

## Switching the patterns

Practice starts D Pattern Looper. While it is mid-cycle, select **Play Change** (or use Change Pattern Looper’s Play). Its amber **QUEUED** indicator remains silent until D completes all 16 beats, including rests. Change begins at that boundary with no extra beat. Select **Play D** mid-cycle to switch back. Both recordings stay available. The practice checkpoint requires both completed application-transport handoffs and observed queued states; demonstrations never count as learner evidence.

During the song, the conductor requests each next chord pattern four beats before its written change. The same application transport and audio scheduler perform the boundary switch. No recording is swapped into another Looper, and there is no automatic round-robin behavior. Cancelling, navigating or exiting stops every owned Looper and pending selection. Temporary demonstration recordings are rolled back to each individual Looper’s prior recording on completion/cancellation.
